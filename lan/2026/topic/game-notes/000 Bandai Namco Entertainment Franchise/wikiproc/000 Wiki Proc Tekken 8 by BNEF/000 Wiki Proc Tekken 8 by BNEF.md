I've recently reached the gold ranks. I should probably be more intentional about my training. Articulating what is happening with video games can be hard. When I started playing, most of what I saw was that stuff was happening, I could not touch my opponents, they rarely even give me a chance to play, I try to mash buttons, but I am always at a disadvantage.

It became apparent that I should at least know what moves I am making. I started with a strategy of memorizing by spam. Checking the big list of attacks is intimidating, but what I can do is just randomly press inputs, and observe the attack. Then I would repeat various inputs I discover over and over again in matches, until eventually I built a habit that the input string produces this attack, and I can visualize in my mind that what attack a pattern of finger movements would bring about.

Tekken players have a shared language to communicate about movements, which now I also try to associate with that mental image:

https://tekken.fandom.com/wiki/Move_Terminology

I'm still improving in this game, and still at the beginning of deliberate practice and study. I guess one tension with video games is I may want to play them to wind down, and not pay a high cost with systematic note taking which makes them seem more serious. But I have been playing this game a while and I do want to improve in a systematic manner, which outweighs that concern.
