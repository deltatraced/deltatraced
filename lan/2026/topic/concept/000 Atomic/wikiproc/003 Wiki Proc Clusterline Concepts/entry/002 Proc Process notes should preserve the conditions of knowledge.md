# Journal

2026-07-05 Wk 27 Sun - 19:16 +03:00

I've had this concept with me for a while now, but let's try to recall what could have originated this concept:

* I began my programming journey by reverse engineering old video games. The task is to find effects in the machine code that can lead us back to what the game developers must have intended. Reverse engineering practice itself is vibrant with note sharing between volunteers. I learned a lot checking the work of others and reading scattered notes and forum correspondences who figure out one new exciting modding direction.
* I loved the concept of gamified learning. When I started university, I remember thinking I want to turn my lessons into games. Through play and experimentation, we expose the original conditions of learning, we turn a contingent lesson into an inevitable pattern to recognize and exploit to win. I didn't get to do this, but I still would love to explore this one day.
* While learning mathematics, I would check online videos to gain intuition. But I noticed that I am often a passive participant in this (contra active participant in gamified learning). Further, I would have liked to see the behind-the-scenes, how was the video researched, and how were the animations put together, and how was the concept studied? This all leads to hoping to see the origins of the products in front of me, which is one of the core reasons I started these open source note repositories, to attempt to expose the origins for all the artifacts I share with the world.
* Philosophic inclination and first-principles reasoning. During studying I've always expressed I rather "understand instead of memorize". Some classes supported this well like mathematics. Others were harder, like history. Knowing the first principles of why things are the way they are can supercharge memory because we can reconstruct the harder to memorize complexity on the fly. It is also something I notice in a lot of philosophic discourse: return to the beginning, and ask why what is must have been. We play with structures we do not understand, there are things we can learn about them through use, but we might mistake them as having always been if we do not also trace their genealogy. Even in mathematics, we might think that people have always added and multiplied like we have been taught since we were children, and thus mistake for reality what is in fact a mental technology with a cultural evolution in human history.
* Functional programming made me love seeing the shape of data, not to just build it imperatively. Then I applied this to my own knowledge work, how can I make it more explicit the shape of knowledge work instead of leaving it fragmented in implicit practice and memory?

Just as I have benefited from reverse engineering the works of others, I wish to maximize the ability for others to reverse engineer my own work.
