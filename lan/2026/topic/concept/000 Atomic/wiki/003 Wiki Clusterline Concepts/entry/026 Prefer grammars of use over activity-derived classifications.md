It can be tempting to classify by project particulars. Maybe we create a folder tree by project component. This can result in a few issues:

1. The classification system we come up with will rarely generalize to other projects. The folders are all particulars of a specific work, and so our notes become more fragmented than they could be.
1. It becomes harder to derive standards in our note taking practice since we are often only concerned with the individual work and not how we may work in the space of all possible such works.

Let's call the above semantics of use. Every work has its own particular shape: Different requirements, data, tools, and connections.

This is in contrast with what we will call grammars of use. Where semantics of use concern the shape of a specific work, grammars of use concern the shape of all possible works. How can we take notes in a way that applies to a *type* of notes rather than just to a particular note in a particular work?

Thinking about grammars of use motivates the development of standards, and gives us additional insight by making distinct works comparable in new ways. This gives us a means by which to assess our note taking ability in time. Problems that arise for us while taking notes on one work may generalize to many similar works, and so we can incrementally observe these tensions and improve our method.

# Examples

For example, this right here is an [artifact](../concept/008%20Term%20Artifact%20Note.md) note with a **note type** of entry. A note type of entry typically is associated with writing, and is differentiated from other types like investigation and task by the fact that it does not require a resolution signal.

Many notes we take may be easily thought of as investigations, issues, policy judgments, or entries. These note types I have created for clusterline were derived as grammars of use, they apply to many possible works and not simply any particular one. This allowed me to reason about [process notes](../concept/000%20Term%20Process%20Note.md) as a kind of entity, even though most of my notes fit that description.

Another example is the Inbox note cluster. It can accumulates and sorts not just TODOs and emails but also scattered questions and points of confusion I have across all my notes that may not be related to my current investigation.

An investigation is a close-ended question with a defined means of resolution, wheras an exploration is an open-ended area of research that can generate new leads.

# Grammars of use are machines

Semantics of use had all of these ways of working implicit. Grammars of use make them explicit and operable. The inbox may have once been simply associated with emails and todos. But now it is a machine that *can* fit those contexts but much more. It has specific rules of working.

The investigation/exploration distinction is also a conceptual machine. Before this distinction, I was always facing the tension of unknown unknowns. Should I commit to asking a concrete question early when I do not know my surroundings? When should I simply play rather than decompose effort? Those are questions that arise in many of my different works, now addressed by this grammar of use for investigations and explorations.

# This is an application of mathematical thinking

This sort of thinking is related to thinking of the generative principles of things rather than their particulars. Think about the mathematical space of all possibilities, and not just the particular ways things connect.

Similar ideas also appear in programming where we express problems algebraically and then compose our work as a specialization of our algebra.

In database modeling, we are also interested in representing the ontology of our system such that it fits many possible data uses. It is possible to commit too early to a very rigid and particular ontology which can make our system less flexible and more frustrating to designers who want to iterate concepts. We can make good tools for designers by giving them grammars of use: Systems that can be used to express their concepts, rather than hardcoding their concept particulars in our systems.
