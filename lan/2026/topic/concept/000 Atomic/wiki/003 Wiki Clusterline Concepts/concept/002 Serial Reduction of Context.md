serial reduction is the process of taking a context and producing a list of next considerations from it.

For example,

The context `Do homework` may be serially reduced to

1. `Write down the problems`
1. `Look up relevant information from the text`
1. `Break down the problem into parts`
1. `Solve problem part 1`
1. `... Do homework`

Here, `Do homework` has been reduced from the left to 4 steps, and we are still within the large context of `5. ... Do homework`.

So serial reduction is more permissive than coming up with a step by step plan that guarantees completion of the task, since the last item is still the broad task. The goal is to start producing next considerations for any given problem. This can help avoid early commitment to breaking a problem too much before we have a clear understanding of the scope and our surroundings. So it is important to be clear about what to do next, and then next, and then next, and so on.

A [Process Note](000%20Term%20Process%20Note.md) was described as a series of logs. It can be useful for these logs to sometimes follow this process of serially reducing the context (as denoted by the note's title). So a note `Do homework` may have multiple timestamped logs of each step being down, and it may have a task section of a multi-step breakdown planned in advance.

serial reduction can help make a task less intimidating by shifting focus from the whole to the layers we are concerned with at the current moment repeatedly.
