# What?

Here I capture some post ideas. In the post [001 Write small posts with a clear goal and audience as inspired by Julia Evans](../../../../../post/001%20Write%20small%20posts%20with%20a%20clear%20goal%20and%20audience%20as%20inspired%20by%20Julia%20Evans.md) I talked about a way of writing posts:

1. Use a full sentence title that centers what you will talk about.
1. Imagine a single person to which the post is speaking to. A fellow peer, a friend, a beginner, etc.

This space is to record ideas as they come that fit the prior constraints. What would I want to tell a friend? What would be cool if other developers tried it?

I'll also specify tags for which the ideas fall under. Ideas can be inlined here chronologically, they can be spawned into their own dedicated note if we want to also do pre-commitment research. post-commitment notes would be found under [005 Post Writing Notes](../../../../../main/entry/005%20Post%20Writing%20Notes/005%20Post%20Writing%20Notes.md).

For whoever is reading, feel free to reach out if any idea here is interesting to you and you would like to make it exist. Feel free to suggest other ideas as well.

The journal has one idea per timestamp card.

`Allow both structure and chaos in your writing: A case for thought streams and explorations.`

`#writing #journaling`

Multiple ideas are converging here. Recently I created a distinction between explorations and investigations across this boundary. With explorations being open-ended play and investigations aim to resolve an investigative question that should be concise, atomic, and resolvable.

A thought stream on the other hand, is just a journal of entries of what comes to mind. It could be indexed by the current day/week, but also it could be indexed by
a specific topic. For example I just installed a new gentoo system, it's a big new experience and I have many practices and thoughts that are forming. This stream of consciousness is valuable and so I record it under [001 Gentoo Usage Thought Stream](../../../../../main/entry/004%20Configuring%20my%20gentoo%20system/entry/001%20Gentoo%20Usage%20Thought%20Stream.md). This is atomic at some level, it will attenuate thought streams very unrelated to using gentoo.

I also keep a private weekly thought stream, which really could be anything that comes to mind I want to process.

Both thought streams and explorations have one thing in common: maximize ease of writing in the moment and foster play and the forming of unexpected connections. Things that we may write in thought streams may seem only secondarily important to us, but they are something that came to mind. We might not otherwise want to dedicate much attention to them, as they feel transient and sidelined. This is a space for them.

`Organising our work by dependency can make it easier to share work with others`

`#programming #sharing-work`

This post is for software development, although aspects of it can probably be adapted elsewhere.

In programming, many of us may end up using the same library or configure the same tool for some purpose.

There has been a movement for sharing configuration amongst developers by creating a `dotfiles`. And resources like with `awesome-*`.

We can add more:

* We can have public notes for errors we run into from specific projects. Stackoverflow and friends do provide a method for us to share errors and solutions amongst one another but personal notes can provide a more end-to-end view of how the error is encountered, which can help others navigate similar problems, rather than only search by error code.
* We can have repositories for examples, indexed by the specific project. These can also be contributed back to the projects themselves under `examples/` if they would like them. But with it being in a public personal project, we can also have many minimal examples of interesting uses that are otherwise too specific to go back to the project. Public notes can reference them to add higher end to end context.
  * An example for project  https://github.com/Peternator7/strum: `git_root/examples/gh/Peternator7/strum/example_crate_1/`
