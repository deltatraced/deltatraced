This is a topic that has shifted my note taking approach in 2026, so I want to write about it, and about the previous system, and the advantages of the modifications introduced.

In my mathematical studies, I benefited greatly from having a structure that guides my proof writing process, such as tracking which cases of a proof I am currently solving, and having the proof being conveyed by symbols rather than linguistic explanations. This brings us to a benefit of structure: It situates readers, They can think through it by allowing it to filter their attention.

I want to keep this space for explaining what's next, and expressing what I think in the moment and where I am at. Currently, I am reviewing prior posts I have written on the importance of process-based note taking. I want to take aspects of this and contrast it with structure-based. Importantly, I want to focus on whether we put immutability and tracing ideas to the foreground or background. There exists a balance we have to strike with process notes. One can overly linearize an idea. Take this very text I am writing here. If I am to treat it as a deliverable, I may be tempted to copy-paste it down, and only modify aspects of it, so as to preserve my developing idea as a chain of different expressing paragraphs. But this is often too micro-level. Important to process notes is that we keep what is important visible. That is, we ought to compress insight. We want to trace a broad path forward, and not be lost in minute details. For me, this paragraph I am writing need not be overly edited. I may make small edits so long as I am currently writing it, but later on, it becomes a trace, just another log, a thought that had made it into writing. So process notes are focused on capture, rather than primarily structure. There is of course some structure that emerges. For example, task graphs are stable entities. Right now I am writing with my focus being primarily about this post, the very title of this note situates me, and this is structure present to process-based notes.

From [000 Note Taking Philosophy](../../../../../../../../../2025/post/000%20Note%20Taking%20Philosophy.md),

 > 
 > Another important criteria to evaluate notes is whether they provide enough information for another creator to reproduce the work, so there is aim for completeness and reproduction of knowledge where applicable.

Let's focus on what we can take away from this, which can crystalize into more concrete notes later.

Notes should help others reproduce a work. If I write a mathematical proof, It helps to present an argument for how the proof was constructed. This can be considered Proof Notes. If I write code, it helps to provide a trace of issues that were solved. This can be considered notes that help reproduce the work. Why did I make a certain decision? All of this is often situated by where the project currently is at. By relation to a specific issue code, a specific milestone or goal code, etc.

This shows that we need to capture a moment-to-moment process in some way. An argument begins with the initial problem, and points your attention to tools that will help solve it. A solution on its own may not preserve those details.

We can also share partial arguments amongst one another, which themselves should encode how far we got in solving a problem.

One thing to note about all this, is that those can all be captured as artifacts themselves. Because they are meant to be shared and collaborated on.

In other words, Process can become deliverable, and thus become mutable. We want to be able to refine our argument. We want to not only capture a *discovery process* but also a *refined discovery process*, to return back to zero, as many times as needed, to once more state what we knew at the beginning, what tools we use to reach our desired conclusions, and what ought be in our attention, and what details bring that to our attention. This is something we can refine. It is a process of problem solving that is itself sharable with others. Thus, unlike this note that I am writing, which can only be mined after I am done with it, an argument is redone, and thus is mutable.

We can have a process note about the argument, to capture aspects of the discovery process of the argument, which is itself a trace of *a* way to discover a solution to a problem.

In [002 Atomic Contexts and Respect](../../../../../../../../../2025/post/002%20Atomic%20Contexts%20and%20Respect.md),

 > 
 > You can think of sequential reduction as a mechanism of decomposing contexts and forming a context cluster from the original intent core context.
 > A complex activity, problem, or task can be intimidating to start. The method of sequential reduction is to take a process and reduce it to a sequence of next steps to take. This also works well with our principle of atomic contexts, as each next significant step becomes its own self-enclosed context.
 > So we ask "What's next?" and our big problem context spawns a context for just the next significant step. Within that context, the process can repeat itself, where its own next significant step can spawn a new context. This forms a branching stack of work, where we create a new context, and when we complete it, we return to its spawner context again and continue where we left off. Gradually, we progress through the original problem sequentially.

This is basically the "what's next" idea, which here is expressed as reducing a problem to a chain of next considerations. This fits nicely with process notes being composed of linear timestamped entries or logs.
